FAQ

Questions people ask

Is this medical advice?

No. It points you to services and gives no diagnosis or treatment. If you are in crisis, call Samaritans on 116 123, text SHOUT to 85258, call NHS 111 and choose option 2, or call 999 in an emergency.

Does it replace the NHS?

No. Your NHS referral stays the main route to treatment. This is for the weeks in between, and it puts the NHS option in your plan alongside other trusted help.

How is it different from the NHS App?

The NHS App is the national route into NHS services. This tool is for the time between asking for help and getting it. Where the NHS App offers something, the Navigator points to it.

What happens to my answers?

Your answers stay in your browser and are not stored or sent anywhere. There are no accounts. If you enter a postcode on the waiting times page, it is sent to the public postcodes.io service to find your area.

Where does the data come from?

Physical health waits come from NHS England referral to treatment data for March 2026. National referral and employment figures come from the NHS Talking Therapies 2025 to 26 annual report. Services come from each organisation's own page. Mental health waits by area are not connected yet.

What does it cost?

Nothing. Some listed services may charge, and each listing shows its cost.

Your rights while you wait

  • Refer yourself to NHS Talking Therapies

    You do not need a GP referral for anxiety and depression. You can refer yourself today through the NHS website or the NHS App.

  • Right to Choose

    For many services you have a legal right to choose a different provider with a shorter wait, including in a neighbouring area. Ask your GP to refer you to the provider you choose.

  • Ask for an urgent review

    If you get worse while waiting, contact the service and your GP. Getting worse can change your priority.

  • In crisis right now

    Call Samaritans on 116 123, free at any time, or text SHOUT to 85258. Call NHS 111 and choose option 2 for the urgent mental health team.

ABOUT

About the Navigator

A free navigation layer for people waiting for NHS mental health support. It connects people to help that already exists and is easy to miss: NHS routes, charity support, peer groups and trusted self help. It does not give medical advice or treatment.

Who built it

Gokul Rajan, Reading. MSc Financial Technology, University of Kent. Product design and data, prototyped with AI coding tools. Not yet reviewed for clinical safety or accessibility, and not an NHS service.

How it fits beside the NHS App

The NHS App is the national route into NHS services. This tool is for the time between asking for help and getting it: a short plan that puts crisis help first, then the NHS route, then trusted charity, peer and self help options. Where the NHS App offers something, the Navigator points to it.

Coverage

Waiting time data covers NHS England, all 42 areas. Scotland, Wales and Northern Ireland run their own health services and are planned for later. Crisis lines and national charities work across the UK. Local listings cover Berkshire West so far.

Data status

  • Physical health waits: real, from the NHS England RTT March 2026 full extract, 184,184 rows and 7.05 million people waiting, as a patient weighted average per area.
  • National referral and employment figures: real, from the NHS Talking Therapies 2025 to 26 annual report.
  • Mental health waits by area: not connected yet. The site says so and shows no estimate.
  • ONS economic inactivity by local authority: not connected yet.
